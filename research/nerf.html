Neural Radiance Fields from Scratch

Implementing the core NeRF pipeline in PyTorch to study how continuous neural scene representations reconstruct 3D appearance from sparse 2D views.

PyTorch Neural Rendering Novel View Synthesis Positional Encoding Volume Rendering

Overview

The goal of this project was to understand NeRF at the implementation level rather than treat it as a black box. I built the core pipeline from scratch in PyTorch, covering camera-ray construction, stratified sampling, multi-frequency positional encoding, neural field prediction, differentiable volume rendering, and optimization.

Method & Implementation

The implementation follows the central NeRF idea: map spatial position and viewing direction to volumetric density and view-dependent color, then render pixels through differentiable alpha compositing.

Scene Representation

Positional Encoding & NeRF MLP

3D positions and viewing directions are expanded with multi-frequency sinusoidal features. An 8-layer MLP with a skip connection predicts volumetric density and view-dependent RGB color.

View core implementation 
class PositionalEncoding(nn.Module):
    def __init__(self, num_frequencies):
        super().__init__()
        self.freq_bands = 2.0 ** torch.linspace(
            0., num_frequencies - 1, num_frequencies
        )

    def forward(self, x):
        out = [x]
        for freq in self.freq_bands:
            out += [torch.sin(x * freq), torch.cos(x * freq)]
        return torch.cat(out, dim=-1)

class NeRFMLP(nn.Module):
    def __init__(self, pos_L=10, dir_L=4, depth=8, width=256):
        super().__init__()
        self.pos_encoder = PositionalEncoding(pos_L)
        self.dir_encoder = PositionalEncoding(dir_L)
        self.skips = [4]
Camera & Geometry

Camera-Aware Views & World-Space Rays

Camera poses are represented with camera-to-world transforms. Pixel coordinates are converted into normalized world-space ray directions, providing the geometric backbone for rendering.

View ray-generation implementation 
def get_rays(H, W, focal, c2w):
    i, j = torch.meshgrid(
        torch.linspace(0, W - 1, W),
        torch.linspace(0, H - 1, H),
        indexing='ij'
    )

    dirs = torch.stack([
        (i.t() - W * 0.5) / focal,
        -(j.t() - H * 0.5) / focal,
        -torch.ones_like(i.t())
    ], dim=-1)

    rays_d = torch.sum(dirs[..., None, :] * c2w[:3, :3], dim=-1)
    rays_d = rays_d / torch.norm(rays_d, dim=-1, keepdim=True)
    rays_o = c2w[:3, 3].expand(rays_d.shape)
Sampling & Rendering

Stratified Sampling & Differentiable Volume Rendering

Samples are drawn along each camera ray, evaluated by the neural field, and composited with transmittance-aware alpha weights. This converts per-sample color and density into a rendered pixel while preserving gradients through the rendering process.

Optimization

Random-Ray Training Loop

Training uses randomly sampled rays rather than repeatedly rendering full-resolution images, reducing compute during experimentation. Adam optimizes an RGB reconstruction objective through the differentiable rendering pipeline.

Evaluation focuses on optimization behavior, reconstruction quality, and the contribution of high-frequency positional encoding.

Training Dynamics & Reconstruction Quality

MSE loss decreases steadily while PSNR rises into the mid-20 dB range, indicating stable convergence and progressively improved reconstruction quality. Late-stage PSNR fluctuations suggest diminishing gains as training approaches saturation.

Positional Encoding Ablation

Removing positional encoding causes the model to collapse toward low-frequency, heavily blurred reconstructions. Multi-frequency encoding substantially improves recovered scene geometry and fine-grained appearance.

Discussion & Limitations

What mattered most

  • Camera geometry: coordinate conventions and ray directions had to be correct before network behavior was meaningful.
  • High-frequency detail: direct coordinates produced overly smooth reconstructions; positional encoding was critical.
  • Compute: random-ray training and lower-resolution previews made iteration more practical.

Current limitations

  • The implementation focuses on the coarse stratified-sampling stage.
  • Hierarchical importance sampling from the original NeRF remains a natural extension.
  • Late-stage reconstruction gains diminish as optimization approaches saturation.
Connection to the original NeRF paper. This project follows the core formulation introduced by Mildenhall et al. (2020): a continuous 5D neural scene representation queried along camera rays and rendered with differentiable volume rendering. The emphasis here is implementation-level understanding of the core pipeline.

Conclusion

The project showed how tightly geometry, representation, sampling, rendering, and optimization interact in neural scene reconstruction. A NeRF is not just an MLP: the full geometric and differentiable-rendering pipeline must work coherently before a meaningful scene representation emerges.
