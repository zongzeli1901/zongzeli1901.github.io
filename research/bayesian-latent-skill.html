01

Research Question

Can repeated pairwise outcomes reveal a useful latent representation of player skill, and can that representation quantify uncertainty rather than collapse each player into a single deterministic score?

A historical win rate is predictive, but it discards who was beaten. The same victory should carry different information depending on the opponent's strength.

This motivates a structured probabilistic view: player skill is latent, match outcomes are observed evidence, and prediction depends on the relative skill difference between two players.

The technical question is therefore not simply whether a Bayesian model can rank players. It is whether its representation produces better held-out probabilities, uses evidence efficiently, and expresses uncertainty in a meaningful way.

Observed Evidence Pairwise win / loss outcomes.
Latent Structure One hidden skill variable per player.
Prediction Pairwise win probability from relative skill.
Uncertainty Posterior width reflects evidential support.
02

Probabilistic Representation

A compact generative model preserves the relational structure of the match graph: latent skills generate relative advantages, which generate observed outcomes.

Latent Prior
sᵢ ~ N(0, 1)

Each player begins with an independent Gaussian prior over unobserved skill.

Pairwise Probability
P(i beats j) = σ(sᵢ − sⱼ)

Prediction depends on relative—not absolute—skill. Opponent strength therefore remains part of the evidence.

Observed Outcome
yᵢⱼ ~ Bernoulli(pᵢⱼ)

Match results connect the hidden representation to observed data through a probabilistic likelihood.

Modeling Rationale

Why model structure matters.

Historical win rate compresses an entire match history into one aggregate number. The latent-variable model instead retains pairwise relational evidence.

Beating a strong opponent and beating a weak opponent therefore induce different evidence about skill, even when both observations are simply recorded as wins.

This gives the model a transparent mathematical mechanism for improving prediction: better representation of the evidence, rather than simply adding model capacity.

Posterior Inference with SVI

The exact posterior over the latent skill vector is not analytically tractable, so inference is converted into an optimization problem through a mean-field variational approximation.

01
Specify the generative model Priors define latent skills; the logistic Bernoulli likelihood links those skills to match outcomes.
02
Construct a variational posterior q(sᵢ)=N(μᵢ,σᵢ²) assigns each player a learned posterior mean and uncertainty.
03
Optimize the ELBO Stochastic Variational Inference balances explanatory fit to observed matches against divergence from the prior.
04
Evaluate unseen outcomes Learned skill estimates are mapped back into pairwise probabilities on 94,007 held-out matches.
Inference Design

Mean-Field Gaussian Guide

Framework Pyro · PyTorch
Variational Family Gaussian
Optimizer Adam
Learning Rate 0.03
SVI Steps 2,000
ELBO = E_q[log p(y|s)] − KL(q(s) || p(s))

The first term rewards explaining observed matches; the KL term regularizes the approximate posterior against the prior.

Data Efficiency

If the representation is using pairwise evidence effectively, useful probabilistic structure should emerge before the full training set is available.

Scaling Behavior

More evidence helps, but the marginal return shrinks.

Accuracy rises from 0.6420 with 25% of the training data to 0.6835 at full scale, while log loss falls from 0.6298 to 0.5850.

The gain is largest at lower data fractions and becomes small from 75% to 100%, consistent with repeated pairwise comparisons progressively constraining relative skill.

With only 25% of the training data, the latent-skill model already reaches 0.6298 log loss— lower than the 0.6619 full-data historical baseline.
07

Posterior Uncertainty

A Bayesian model should not hide weak evidence behind a precise-looking point estimate. Posterior width provides a direct way to inspect how much information supports each inferred skill.

Bly · 4,933 matches Dense evidence → sharply concentrated posterior.
Albion · 33 matches Sparse evidence → substantially wider posterior.
Posterior Sanity Check

Observation frequency is reflected in posterior width.

Posterior uncertainty behaves consistently with the amount of available evidence: frequent players receive sharply concentrated posteriors, while rare players remain substantially more uncertain.

The importance of this result is not that Bayesian inference should produce narrower posteriors with more data—that is expected. Rather, the experiment shows that uncertainty itself becomes diagnostic information: the model exposes where the observed evidence is insufficient instead of hiding that limitation behind a single deterministic score.

This shifts the next research question from whether skill can be inferred to what latent or contextual factors explain the uncertainty that remains. Race, map, game version, and time-varying ability are natural candidates for extending the model.

More observations → narrower posterior.
Remaining uncertainty points toward missing structure in the current model, not merely prediction error.

09

Research Limits

A credible probabilistic analysis also requires being precise about what these experiments do not establish.

Supported

What the current experiments show

  • Better held-out accuracy and log loss than the selected baselines.
  • Improved log loss as more pairwise evidence becomes available.
  • A qualitative relationship between observation count and posterior width.
  • A transparent probabilistic mechanism for incorporating opponent strength.
Not Yet Established

What would require additional experiments

  • Formal probability calibration; log loss alone is not a calibration test.
  • Robustness to distribution shift across game versions, maps, or time periods.
  • Whether mean-field SVI is the best posterior approximation for this task.
  • Agreement between inferred skill rankings and external tournament-level ground truth.

Next Research Directions

The next step is to preserve the model's transparent probabilistic structure while testing richer latent factors and stronger inference diagnostics.

Dynamic Skill Replace a fixed skill scalar with a time-varying latent process to capture improvement and decline.
Contextual Latent Effects Separate player skill from race, version, map, and other match-specific sources of variation.
Inference Comparison Compare SVI against a TrueSkill-style expectation-propagation approach to study posterior approximation quality.
Calibration & Reliability Add reliability diagrams, calibration error, and external ranking checks to test whether posterior confidence is trustworthy.
